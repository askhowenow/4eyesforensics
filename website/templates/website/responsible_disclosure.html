Responsible disclosure policy

Found something
in our systems?

We test other people's systems for a living, so it would be absurd to pretend ours are perfect. If you have found a weakness in something we run, tell us. This page is the promise we make in return: an answer from a human, a decision you can hold us to, and no legal threat for acting in good faith.

Acknowledgement
1 day
A named engineer replies within one business day. Not an autoresponder.
Triage decision
5 days
In scope or out, with our reasoning, and a severity we will justify.
Fix target
90 days
Critical and high issues sooner. We will tell you if it will slip, and why.
Credit
Yours
Named on this page if you want it, anonymous if you prefer. Your choice, not ours.
01 · Scope

What is ours to fix

In scope
  • 4eyesforensics.com and any subdomain we operate
  • The client portal, including its authentication and session handling
  • Our one-time upload service and the links it issues
  • Our published OpenPGP key handling and this policy's own claims
  • Anything that could expose one client's data to another

That last one we treat as critical by default. Tenant isolation is the finding we most often report to others; we would rather hear about ours from you than from a client.

Out of scope
  • Our clients' systems. Never test them, under any circumstances
  • Third-party services we merely consume, report those to their owner
  • Denial of service, load testing, or anything degrading availability
  • Social engineering of our staff, suppliers or clients
  • Physical intrusion, or anything touching evidence in our custody
  • Scanner output with no demonstrated impact, and missing-header reports

If you believe you have found an exposure belonging to one of our clients, do not test it and do not tell anyone else. Contact us and we will route it to them under their own disclosure process, with you credited.

02 · Rules

Good faith, defined

Vague safe-harbour language protects the company, not the researcher. So here is what we actually mean, in terms you can rely on.

01

Stop at proof

Demonstrate the issue with the minimum access required, then stop. Do not pivot, escalate for its own sake, or go looking for what else the flaw reaches.

02

Do not keep what you find

If you encounter client data, stop immediately and tell us in the report. Do not download, retain, screenshot beyond what proves the point, or share it. Delete it once we confirm receipt.

03

Leave nothing behind

No persistence, no backdoors, no accounts, no uploaded files. If you created anything to prove the issue, list it so we can remove it.

04

Give us ninety days

Publish after ninety days, or sooner if we have fixed it and agreed. If we are moving too slowly, say so and we will either move faster or explain ourselves. We will not ask you to stay quiet indefinitely.

05

No extortion

Reports conditioned on payment, or accompanied by a deadline and a threat, are handled as extortion rather than disclosure. We do not run a paid bounty; see below.

Our side of it
  • We will not pursue legal action, or support any pursued by others, against anyone who follows this policy in good faith
  • We will not report you to your employer, your university, or a regulator
  • We will tell you what we fixed and when, not just that it is closed
  • Every report is reviewed by two of our engineers before we call it out of scope. Four eyes applies to reports about us, too

If you are unsure whether something is permitted, ask first. A question costs you an email and costs us nothing.

On payment

We do not operate a paid bug bounty, and we would rather say so plainly than imply one. What we do offer: public credit if you want it, a written account of the fix, and a straight reference for anyone who asks. Several researchers who reported to us are now people we call when we need a second opinion.

03 · Reporting

How to send it

Encrypt anything containing a working exploit, a credential, or client data. For everything else, plain email to the same address is fine and we will not hold it against you.

01

Email secure@4eyesforensics.com

Subject line starting DISCLOSURE routes it straight to the duty engineer rather than the sales queue.

02

Encrypt to our key

Verify the fingerprint against /.well-known/security.txt or by phone before first use. Never trust a fingerprint quoted in a document, an email signature, or on this page alone.

03

If it is already being exploited

Do not wait for email. Call the incident line, +1 876 555 0142, answered in person day or night. Say it is a disclosure and you will reach an engineer, not a receptionist.

What makes a report easy to act on
  • The affected URL, host or endpoint, exactly
  • Steps to reproduce, in order, that we can follow without guessing
  • The request and response that prove it, redacted of any real data
  • What an attacker gains, in one sentence. This drives severity more than anything else
  • Dates and times you tested, so we can match it to our logs
  • How you want to be credited, or that you would rather not be

No template to fill in. A clear paragraph and a reproduction beats a form every time, and we would rather have the report roughly written than not at all.
